# Portfolio case study: The Bartlett Record

**Project lead:** Robert King  
**Work:** AI-assisted public-record synthesis, financial reconciliation, evidence management and reproducible civic research.  
**Release:** September 28, 2026.

## Problem

Public discussion combined nonprofit cash flow, annual accounting, municipal premises, grant administration, corporate decisions and political messaging. Treating those as one story obscured the separate evidence requirements.

## Work performed

Built a dated dossier separating primary documents, party statements, calculations, provisional transcriptions and unresolved hypotheses. Reconciled the contribution-category change, distinguished accounting results from cash, tested rental arithmetic, preserved contrary evidence, identified existing-record acquisition routes, and designed a source-first witness protocol. The consolidated distribution includes reports, workbooks, CSV/JSON data, source inventories and hash manifests. Native City documents were acquired through a logged, read-only workflow; failed IRS downloads remain explicitly unacquired.

## Reusable portfolio description

Developed a source-linked civic research dossier integrating nonprofit financial statements, municipal lease and grant records, regulatory materials and political-message provenance. Built reproducible reconciliations and versioned evidence tables; corrected unsupported causal claims; prepared privacy-aware independent-verification handoffs.

## Limitations are part of the work

The project does not establish an unlawful takeover, an authenticated funding-for-departure bargain, a survey commissioner, or a final current operator. It did not conduct witness interviews or submit government-record requests simply by preparing templates. The researcher is not presented as a government auditor, lawyer or independent witness. Publicity and recipient response are not measures of factual truth.

## Methods

Actor → resource → authority → condition → action → consequence. Separate event, knowledge, publication and legal-effective dates. Validate arithmetic and file identity, without treating those tests as proof of the sources' truth. Preserve rejected explanations and correct them explicitly.
